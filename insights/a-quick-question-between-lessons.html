A quick question between lessons: what teachers really need from an AI helper

Gaps in a school day are short. A useful helper fits that gap, answers one question at a time, and leaves the decision with you.

6 October 2026 · 6 min read · Facts as of 6 October 2026

What a school day looks like

Your day is not planned around apps. It is planned around periods, bells and the staff room. Your questions are small and exact. How do I explain equivalent fractions to a Class 6 group that still finds fractions hard? How do I describe photosynthesis in Marathi so it does not sound like a textbook read aloud?

These questions come just before a lesson, or in the few minutes between two classes. To find a first answer, you may need to look through several books. Or you wait for a colleague to be free.

The problem

Most general AI chat apps were not made for that short gap. They expect a laptop, good internet and time for a long chat. Their replies sound smooth. That is the problem. A smooth reply that is wrong looks just like a smooth reply that is right.

You do not need an app that sounds sure of itself. You need one that is quick to ask and quick to read or hear. And it must be honest about where its reply came from.

Our view

This is the company view behind VIDYA Copilot. It comes from our product papers and from how our CEO, Divya Prakash Jha, explains why we made the app. A teacher helper must do three things at once. It must fit the moment. It must leave you in control. And it must be honest about what it cannot do. If it misses any one of these, it stops being useful in a real school.

You can see this view in small choices in the app, not in slogans.

The choices we made

One question at a time. The app treats every question as new. You put the class, the topic and what you need in the question. Then you get a reply. There is no long chat to scroll through. The app does not remember what you asked before. This fits a two-minute gap.

Type or speak. Read or listen. You can type your question or tap the microphone. You can read quietly in the staff room with the sound off. Or you can turn on spoken replies, and stop them any time. The phone can start reading aloud before the whole reply is written. So you do not wait for a big block of text.

Replies are written on the phone. The app’s AI is saved on the Android phone. The phone writes the reply itself. The app is not allowed to use the internet. Listening and speaking use your phone’s own tools. These may need internet unless the right language files are on your phone. That is why we say this next to every voice claim on this website.

A warning you can see. Every reply in the regular app shows this line: “Answered from memory, not from your book — worth checking.” This means the app did not look at your textbook. The line is not saying sorry. It tells you how the reply was made, so you know what to do with it.

What the tests show

Our reports cover early versions on test phones. In one test of the newer AI version, with the app already ready, it began speaking about 434 milliseconds (less than half a second) after the question was sent. That was one test on one phone, not a promise. In the same tests, about one in ten replies were hard to make sense of. Results were worse on topics the app does not know well, and in Marathi.

These two facts go together. The app is made to reply quickly. And you still need to check its replies. One fact does not cancel the other.

The honest result

No one has shown yet that VIDYA Copilot saves teachers a set amount of time. No one has shown that it improves results. What the app gives you today is smaller, but useful. It is one more place to ask a school question in Hindi, Marathi or English. You can read or hear the reply, then check it against your book.

What to do next

Do you teach Classes 6 to 8 and have an Android phone? Then start by asking the app a few questions you already know the answers to. Are the replies clear? Are they in the right language? Are they worth checking, not worth ignoring? If yes, the app has earned a place between your lessons. If not, you lost ten minutes and learned something.

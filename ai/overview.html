VIDYA Copilot: a short overview

What it is: VIDYA Copilot is an Android phone app where a teacher asks a school question, by typing or speaking, and gets a short reply in Hindi, Marathi or English to check before using. The teacher can read the reply or hear it aloud. Listening and speaking use your phone's own tools. These may need internet unless the right language files are on your phone. The teacher checks the reply and decides whether to use it. On the phone, the app shows the name VIDYA Shikshak. This website calls the same app VIDYA Copilot.

Brand line: A helping hand for teachers. You make the final decision.

Who it is for: School teachers in India, mainly those who teach Classes 6–8. Also school coordinators and leaders who are thinking about a small trial with teachers. It is not an app for students. VIDYA AI is a different app for students, with its own website.

Where it stands: Early version. It is not on the Play Store yet. To get the app, contact the team. Prices for teachers, setup and school trials have not been announced. We do not claim any user numbers, reviews, certificates or partners.

How it works

  • The app's AI is saved on the Android phone. The phone writes the reply itself. The app is not allowed to use the internet.
  • Replies come from what the app's AI has learned. The app does not search the teacher's textbook. A part that checks replies against the textbook was tested. It is turned off in the regular app.
  • Every reply shows this warning: "Answered from memory, not from your book — worth checking." This means the app did not look at the textbook.
  • Listening and speaking use your phone's own tools. These may need internet unless the right language files are on your phone. These tools also have their own privacy choices.
  • Each question is treated as new. The app does not remember earlier questions.
  • The teacher's details (name, school, classes, subjects) are saved on the phone. The app does not connect to any school system.

What our tests found (one set of tests, one test phone)

  • With the app already ready, it began speaking about 434 ms (less than half a second) after the question was sent. Listening time is not included. This was tested on an OPPO phone. It is not a promise for every phone or question.
  • About one in ten replies were hard to make sense of. Replies were worse on less common topics. Marathi replies needed the most work.
  • We have not finished tests on low-cost phones. We have not finished checks with screen readers (tools that read the screen aloud). We have not shared exact phone needs yet. An iPhone version is not confirmed.

What the regular app cannot do yet

  • Using textbook facts to write replies (built, turned off).
  • Suggesting marks with a marking guide (built for testing, turned off, not good enough yet).
  • Reading photos of handwritten answer papers (tests were not good enough).
  • Class study reports or a school information page (planned).
